# Live Alone — Website Creative Brief

## Current direction — September 2026

This direction supersedes the earlier visual treatments described below.
Build the identity from real band material: photography, the official video,
release artwork, and specific shared history. Avoid numbered section labels,
invented poetic headings, simulated grain/paper, and a repeated giant
sans-serif/italic-serif section formula. Keep navigation quiet, show the logo
at the opening rather than in persistent UI, and keep missing show dates small.
The painting for **In From the Cold (2024)** should supply the color itself.

The biography may draw on Beggs and Neal meeting in kindergarten, the members'
shared childhood and electronic music through Nuschi, Live Alone taking shape
around 2021, and its increasingly collaborative writing. The band is preparing
an untitled six-song EP, with recording planned for December; this is a plan,
not a released record. Preserve accessible navigation and the video player
that loads only after a visitor chooses to play it.

Official website for:

# LIVE ALONE

---

# Core Concept

The Live Alone website should feel like an extension of the band's music rather than simply a place where information is stored.

The website should feel:

- dark
- intimate
- cinematic
- emotional
- atmospheric
- imperfect
- DIY
- slightly damaged
- contemporary without feeling trendy

A useful guiding phrase is:

> Beautiful from a distance. Damaged when you look closer.

The approximate balance of the visual identity should be:

**65% atmospheric / shoegaze**

**35% abrasive / post-hardcore**

The result should feel emotionally heavy without becoming visually chaotic.

---

# Design Philosophy

The most important principle for the website is:

# CLEAN FIRST. DAMAGED SECOND.

The underlying website should be built using strong typography, spacing, hierarchy, composition, and usability.

Texture and distortion should then be layered selectively over that foundation.

If everything looks distorted, distortion stops feeling meaningful.

Important content should always remain readable.

---

# Things the Website Should Feel Like

Useful visual references include:

- late-1990s and early-2000s music culture
- independent record sleeves
- photocopied show flyers
- old music magazines
- VHS recordings
- damaged video tape
- darkroom photography
- handwritten journals
- DIY venue photography
- printed zines
- modern editorial web design

The site should feel like these ideas have been filtered through a modern, responsive website.

---

# Things to Avoid

Do not make the website feel like:

- a technology startup
- a SaaS landing page
- a corporate portfolio
- a generic Squarespace musician template
- an EDM festival page
- a polished influencer website
- a luxury fashion store
- a heavily animated design demo

Avoid excessive:

- rounded cards
- glowing gradients
- pill-shaped buttons
- glassmorphism
- neon UI elements
- generic icon grids
- enormous UI animations

Live Alone should feel like a band.

Not a software product.

---

# Color Palette

The foundation should primarily use:

- near-black
- charcoal
- off-white
- cool gray

Possible accent colors include:

- cold blue
- faded cobalt
- desaturated blue-gray
- muted red
- dirty white
- occasional green/yellow tones from live photography

Album artwork and photography may temporarily introduce stronger colors.

Large sections should generally remain dark enough that those moments of color feel intentional.

Pure white may sometimes feel too sterile.

Prefer slightly warmer or dirtier off-white tones where appropriate.

---

# Typography

Typography should combine two visual languages.

## Primary Typography

Primary interface typography should be:

- highly readable
- bold
- editorial
- slightly condensed when appropriate
- confident
- simple

Good uses include:

- navigation
- section titles
- show dates
- buttons
- body text
- release information

Typography should often use uppercase treatment and generous spacing.

---

## Secondary Typography

A secondary handwritten or distressed style may be used sparingly.

It should feel:

- scribbled
- urgent
- handwritten
- emotionally unstable
- similar to something written quickly in a musician's notebook

This treatment works well for:

- song titles
- small annotations
- decorative text
- visual overlays

Do not use distressed typography for long paragraphs.

---

# Texture

Possible textures include:

- subtle film grain
- photocopy noise
- paper texture
- scratches
- VHS interference
- horizontal tracking lines
- halftone patterns
- blurred highlights
- analog bloom
- very slight image misalignment

Textures should usually exist as secondary visual layers.

They should support the design rather than obscure it.

---

# Photography Direction

Current photography includes compressed Instagram images.

This limitation should be embraced rather than hidden.

Photography can intentionally feel archival, photocopied, damaged, or documentary.

Two visual modes should be used throughout the site.

## Mode One — Editorial Black and White

Black-and-white images should feel:

- cold
- distant
- documentary
- intimate
- slightly confrontational

These work particularly well for:

- About
- band introduction
- section transitions

---

## Mode Two — Saturated Performance Photography

Live photography may retain dramatic colors.

Examples include:

- green stage lighting
- yellow lighting
- red lighting
- blown highlights
- dark silhouettes

These images provide bursts of energy within the otherwise restrained site.

The contrast between monochrome portraiture and saturated performance imagery should become part of the site's visual identity.

---

The initial website should primarily be a single long scrolling homepage.

Navigation should smoothly lead users to the primary sections.

Navigation should remain minimal.

Possible structure:

MUSIC

The hero should create the strongest first impression on the site.

It should likely fill most or all of the initial viewport.

Possible content:

LIVE ALONE

PORTLAND, OREGON

BUT I DON'T

OFFICIAL VIDEO

[ WATCH VIDEO ]

The hero may eventually use:

- a cinematic still from the "But I Don't" video
- a band photograph
- a short optimized looping video

Possible effects include:

- very slow zoom
- film grain
- tiny exposure fluctuations
- subtle VHS instability
- occasional text distortion

Effects must remain subtle.

The hero should feel cinematic rather than animated.

# About

The About section should contain:

- one strong band photograph
- a concise band biography

The biography should likely be approximately 100–200 words.

The section should feel personal and editorial rather than promotional.

Avoid exaggerated marketing language.

A black-and-white group photograph is a strong candidate for this section.

---

# Photography / Visual Interlude

Photography does not necessarily need to exist inside a conventional "Gallery" section.

Instead, photographs can occasionally interrupt the page.

Possible arrangements:

- offset images
- overlapping photographs
- irregular crops
- large single images
- contact-sheet inspired layouts
- narrow vertical photographs beside oversized text

Possible material includes:

- live photography
- rehearsal photographs
- band portraits
- music video stills
- artwork

The page should never become visually cluttered simply to display more photographs.

# Image Treatment

Compressed Instagram photographs should not be aggressively sharpened.

Instead, their limitations can be intentionally incorporated into the aesthetic.

Possible treatment:

- monochrome conversion
- contrast adjustments
- grain
- cropping
- halftone
- dark overlays
- photocopy-style processing

Faces and recognizable features should remain intact.

---

# Motion

Motion should be slow and understated.

Good examples:

- grain movement
- gentle image zoom
- slow text reveal
- subtle hover movement
- temporary VHS displacement

Bad examples:

- constant shaking
- aggressive glitch effects
- dramatic page transitions
- excessive parallax
- spinning graphics
- large autoplay animations

The site's emotional weight should come primarily from composition, photography, and typography.

---

# User Experience

The site should be understandable immediately.

A first-time visitor should quickly be able to answer:

Who is this band?

What do they sound like?

What is their newest release?

Where can I listen?

Are they playing shows?

How can I contact them?

Visual experimentation should never interfere with those questions.

---

# Mobile Experience

The website must feel intentional on phones.

Avoid simply stacking desktop elements.

Mobile design should consider:

- shorter headlines
- image cropping
- touch-friendly navigation
- reasonable text sizes
- loading performance
- video behavior
- simplified visual effects

A significant portion of music discovery happens through mobile social platforms, so the mobile version is extremely important.

---

# Performance

The site should feel fast despite the visual effects.

Prefer:

- optimized images
- lazy-loaded media
- CSS effects
- lightweight textures
- static fallbacks
- restrained JavaScript

Avoid requiring a visitor to download multiple enormous media files before seeing the page.

---

# Overall Emotional Direction

The website should feel like entering Live Alone's visual world.

It should feel somewhat cold and distant initially, but reveal more emotion and disorder as the visitor moves through the page.

The combination should be:

beauty + discomfort

clarity + damage

distance + intimacy

atmosphere + aggression

The final site should feel unmistakably connected to Live Alone.
